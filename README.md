## Youfeng Xu

Those two halves usually sit in different people. Holding both means the question I keep returning to is the
awkward one: **how would we know?** Not whether a system is impressive in a demonstration, but whether the number
underneath the decision means what it appears to mean, and whether the institution is equipped to act on it.

Director of Programme Management at GovTech, consulting to agencies on technology and AI transformation.

### AI evaluation and assurance

When a benchmark reports that a system scored 48%, what is that number made of? More of it than anyone reports is
credit a system doing nothing would also earn — and that is measurable, from artefacts benchmarks already publish.

- Six non-performing policies through the unmodified harnesses of fifteen public agent benchmarks. On six, a
  policy that never acts ranks at or above every published system on some reported column.
- Replaying benchmarks' own published trajectories with every action deleted, separating credit earned from credit
  conferred — plus a rule that estimates the same quantity from logs, with no replay harness.
- Whether two LLM reviewers drawn from the same model share blind spots, on a corpus with planted defects where
  ground truth is known by construction.
- What separates models that score the same: termination, calibration, and which classes of instruction they fail.

Write-ups are in preparation. Code and data are released alongside them.

### The information environment

Computational models of online audience behaviour — generative AI, psychographic profiling and social network
analysis — used to simulate how information campaigns propagate and to design interventions on evidence rather
than intuition.

- *Look What You Made Us Cluster: Hate Narrative Extraction from Reddit Discourse* (2026), with DSO National
  Laboratories and DSTA. Narratives extracted as entity–evaluation pairs rather than topic clusters, so what is
  recovered is what is being said about whom, not merely what is being discussed.
